# Writing rules

Rules every GTM Studio writer follows, whatever the network. Network-specific rules live in `networks/<network>/playbook.md`. brand.md always wins over this file.

## The only source of facts is brand.md

- Features, numbers, prices, limits, integrations and claims come from brand.md, sections 4 and 5. If a sentence needs a fact that is not there, rewrite the sentence without it. Never invent a statistic, a customer, a quote or a result.
- Use the product's real words: the names of screens, plans and features as they appear in brand.md.
- Examples must be concrete: a role, a situation, a before and an after. "A sales rep pastes the job post and gets three rewritten bullet points" beats "users save time".

## Voice

- Write in the content language given in brand.md, with the register brand.md sets (tú/usted, first person singular or plural, humor, emoji policy).
- Follow the tone adjectives and the "say it like this / never like this" examples. Read them before writing and again before finishing.
- Short sentences. Concrete nouns and verbs. One idea per line. Cut every adjective that does not carry information.
- Address one reader, the ideal customer from brand.md. Not "everyone", not "companies".

## Hooks (the first two lines)

- Lines 1 and 2 together stay under the network's `hookChars` (210 for LinkedIn). They must make the reader want line 3.
- Hooks that work: a specific number; a scene the reader has lived ("Tuesday, 11pm, the third rewrite of the same CV"); a plain contrarian statement backed later in the post; a question the reader has actually asked themselves; the cost of the problem in time or money.
- Hooks that do not: the product name; a greeting; "I'm excited to announce"; "Did you know…?"; a definition; anything the reader cannot picture.
- Line 3 is blank.

## Forbidden

- The forbidden words and phrases in brand.md section 8, without exception.
- AI-sounding fillers in any language: "in today's world", "en el mundo actual", "revolutionary", "revolucionario", "game-changer", "discover how", "descubre cómo", "it's not just X, it's Y", "no es solo X, es Y", "unlock", "seamless", "cutting-edge", "elevate", "supercharge", "dive in", "let that sink in", "imagine a world", "at the end of the day", "en un mundo donde", "sin duda", "a día de hoy".
- Lists where every line starts with an emoji. Three adjectives in a row. Two exclamation marks in one post. "Agree?" or "Thoughts?" as the closing line.
- Em-dashes as the default punctuation. Use a period or a comma.
- Promises about outcomes the product does not control ("you will get hired").
- Competitor names, unless brand.md section 10 allows comparisons.

## Calls to action

- One ask per post, stated plainly, with what happens after the click.
- Where the link goes is decided once, in brand.md's tokens (`linkPlacement`), never by the writer:
  - `first-comment` (the LinkedIn default: links in the body lower reach): no URL in the post; the post says "link in the first comment" in the content language; the checklist carries the comment text with the URL.
  - `body`: the URL is the last line before the hashtags; the checklist's first comment is a short follow-up without repeating the pitch.
- `cta` posts ask directly. Other goals end with a soft next step or nothing at all.

## Hashtags

- Between the network's min and max (3 to 5 on LinkedIn), on their own last line, specific to the niche, the role or the problem. No generic ones (#motivation, #success, #ai).

## Self-check before saving

1. Lines 1 and 2 hook and fit the character limit. Line 3 is blank.
2. Every fact traces back to brand.md.
3. No forbidden word or phrase. Search the text for each one.
4. Length within the format's target. Hashtag count within limits.
5. The post reads aloud naturally in the content language. No translated-sounding sentences.
6. For carousels: each slide holds one idea; slide titles are 3 to 10 words; the cover is the hook; the last slide is the ask.
7. The checklist says exactly which files to upload and when to post.
